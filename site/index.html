Your sidebar, your Spaces, your command bar. NotArc is a free, open-source browser for Mac that keeps the way Arc works.

Why “NotArc”?

Because it isn't Arc. It's a new browser, written from scratch in Objective-C++ on Chromium (CEF), so sites work like they do in Chrome. It contains none of Arc's code, artwork or branding.

Volunteers make it, it's MIT licensed, and it has nothing to do with The Browser Company. We liked how Arc worked and didn't want that to go away.

Sidebar

Your tabs live on the side.

Favorites sit at the top as icon tiles. Below them, Pinned tabs and folders, nested and sorted by drag and drop. Today tabs go underneath.

Spaces

One window, several lives.

Each Space has its own browser profile, so work logins stay at work and personal ones stay home. Give it a colour or a two-colour gradient with grain, and an emoji icon.

Command bar

Cmd+T, then type.

Enter goes to an address or searches. Along the way it suggests open and pinned tabs from every Space, your history and menu commands, and it learns what you pick.

Search with Google, DuckDuckGo, Bing, Ecosia or Kagi.

⌘TOpen the command bar

Split view

Up to four pages at once.

Drag a tab onto the page to split it. Keep going until four sit side by side.

Little Window and Peek

Links from elsewhere stay out of the way.

Links from other apps open in a Little Window instead of a new tab. Links that leave a pinned site open in Peek, an overlay over the page you were on.

⌘⌥NLittle Window, from any app
Auto-archive

Old tabs tidy themselves.

Today tabs you haven't used for 12 hours move to a searchable Archive. Pick anything from 6 hours to 30 days, or never.

Bring your Arc with you.

On first launch NotArc imports what you had in Arc. The rest is one menu item away.

It only reads Arc's files. It never changes them.

  • On first launchYour Arc Spaces, tabs, Favorites, archive and logins.
  • File > Import Everything from ArcAlso brings history, extensions, link rules, saved passwords, addresses and cards.
  • From other browsersBookmarks, history and passwords from Chrome, Brave, Edge, Vivaldi, Firefox and Safari.

Also in there.

  • Passwords saved and filled in, behind Touch ID
  • Max: ask about a page or summarise it, on your Mac
  • Chrome extensions, from the Web Store
  • Air Traffic Control link rules
  • Media controls and picture-in-picture
  • Capture, an area or the full page
  • Boosts: per-site colours, fonts and zaps
  • Notes
  • Easels with live clips and drawing
  • Live Folders for RSS, GitHub and YouTube
  • Next meeting from your Mac's Calendar
  • Multiple windows
  • Incognito windows
  • AppleScript
  • Automatic updates, checked against a published SHA-256

The parity checklist tracks what's done and what isn't.
